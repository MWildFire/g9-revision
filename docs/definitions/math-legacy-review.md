# Legacy mathematics definition review

The English and Russian legacy lessons were reviewed in place: definitions, examples, practice answers and interactive explanations. The exact primary count is **55 named teaching records**: 51 definition/method cards and four prose extensions. Some records define several terms, and some methods revisit the same concept. This is not a claim of 55 distinct mathematical objects.

Thirteen simulation descriptions repeat those topics. Ten simulation components contain graphs or geometry; three are calculators. Four primary cards include structured labelled diagrams. Eight mock lessons contain 22 explanatory paragraphs supported by 14 grouped review annotations. All 68 practice problems were reviewed.

All wording is an authored paraphrase. Source-language labels identify evidence, not quotation language. Some Russian records have independently read Russian corroboration for part of their content; others are authored adaptations of English sources. A Russian link does not imply that every claim in a grouped record was independently sourced in Russian.

## Conventions and evidence

Natural numbers start at1 in the number-set activity. Functions distinguish domain, codomain and attained range. Principal roots and exponent rules state real-domain restrictions. Quartiles use medians of halves, excluding an odd central median. Grouped interpolation explicitly assumes uniform distribution within each class. Standard deviation distinguishes population and sample formulas. Bearings are measured clockwise from north; this site normalises a complete turn to000° rather than keeping an equivalent360° value.

Bounds intervals, within-class interpolation and segment-area subtraction are authored derivations from cited rules and stated assumptions. The РЭШ exponential lesson was read through indexed text; direct access timed out, and OpenStax independently supports the mathematical definition. The four criteria-trainer descriptions are local activity summaries, checked against the IB2021 Subject Brief, sectionIII; they are not a complete assessment rubric or a claim about a current school syllabus.
